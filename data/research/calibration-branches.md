# Calibration evidence: suspected occult scaphoid injury

These external studies can inform authored branch ranges after clinical selection. They do not estimate unconditional fracture probability for all adults with a negative wrist radiograph, and they are not RWJ observations. The populations differ by examination findings, referral for MRI, availability of early MRI, CT workup, and follow-up. Rates below retain the source numerator and denominator; calculated values, where provided, are labeled as calculations.

## Evidence and bounded uses

- **MRI yield in an ED-selected cohort.** In a prospective single-centre UK MRI pathway, 27/208 completed scans showed occult scaphoid fracture (source-reported 13%; calculated 12.98%). Other-bone fractures were 45/208 (22%); scaphoid contusions 13/208 (6%); other contusion/ligament injury 42/208 (20%); solely degenerative findings 21/208 (10%); and normal scans 60/208 (29%). These six mutually exclusive outcome categories, including the 27 scaphoid fractures, sum to 208. The separate associated-injury table should not be confused with this MRI category distribution. The cohort enrolled for 12 months from end September 2018 to end September 2019; it included patients aged 12 and older and recorded outcomes at a minimum of one year, with EHR review in January 2021. Only 258 of 337 assessed patients were referred for MRI, so this describes a selected MRI group, not all negative films. [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8244792/).
- **Discharge and return.** The same cohort reports 100 MRI patients discharged from ED with advice and one re-presentation. Methods describe minimum one-year outcomes and an EHR search in January 2021; the exact discharge-specific observation window is not stated. Treat this as a reported count only; it cannot calibrate time-specific return or missed-fracture probability.
- **Randomized immediate-MRI pathway.** SMaRT enrolled patients aged 16 or older with examination/history consistent with suspected scaphoid injury and negative four-view radiographs. By three months, scaphoid fracture was diagnosed in 7/67 intervention participants (10.4%) and 4/65 controls (6.2%); the groups were not significantly different (p=0.37). Non-scaphoid fractures were 15/67 (22.4%) and 5/65 (7.7%). Four participants were lost to follow-up; five intervention participants did not receive MRI. The results compare pathways and cannot be read as a causal individual-level probability. [Primary trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6681676/).
- **Earlier randomized pathway.** A 84-person ED trial reported scaphoid fractures in 3/45 early-MRI participants (6.7%) and 4/39 clinic-reassessment controls (10.3%). Mean clinic appointments were 1.1 ± 0.5 versus 2.3 ± 0.8 (p=0.001). This small, single-centre trial supports a pathway-utilization comparator; it does not establish a return-visit or failed-follow-up rate. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22505295/).
- **Second-line MRI after negative CT.** In a prospective diagnostic cohort of 100 patients with suspected scaphoid fracture and negative radiographs and CT, the abstract reports any fracture in 16% (15 patients), scaphoid fracture in 8%, bone bruise in 10%, and hand-surgery referral for 25% of those with fracture or bruising. The count 15/100 calculates to 15%, conflicting with the abstract’s 16%; referral numerator and denominator are not supplied, so neither is resolved or converted into counts. This highly selected CT-negative group must remain separate from the initial negative-radiograph branch. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34918571/).

## Modeling boundary and gaps

Use conditional branches such as “persistent clinical suspicion after negative films → clinician-selected MRI/CT/repeat imaging” and “MRI result → occult scaphoid / other injury / normal.” Do not sample an unconditional fracture probability from any row. Referral and discharge were pathway decisions, not universal rules. The return record has minimum one-year cohort ascertainment, but not a standardized discharge-specific return window. None of these sources supplies a robust rate for missed follow-up, unplanned ED return over a stated interval, or discharge-versus-referral among a comparable US hospital cohort. Keep those probabilities unspecified until local, appropriately defined data are available.
