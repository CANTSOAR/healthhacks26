# Insurance evidence and modeling boundaries

Six structured extracts are in [specialty-insurance.json](specialty-insurance.json), including one original multicenter cohort on insurance-related post-acute discharge delays. Sources are [Aetna precertification](https://www.aetna.com/health-care-professionals/precertification.html), [Aetna2025 Medicare Advantage metrics](https://www.aetna.com/content/dam/aetna/pdfs/wwwaetnamedicarecomSSL/individual/website/2025_PA_Metric_Report.pdf), [CMS prior-authorization final-rule summary](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/cms-interoperability-prior-authorization-final-rule-cms-0057-f) and [CMS emergency-care rights](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/your-patient-rights/emergency-room-rights).

The Aetna sample is Medicare Advantage contract H0523 only, not a representative Aetna-wide or RWJ payer mix. On physical PDF pages 1–2, standard requests show 97.61% approved, 2.39% denied, and 53.33% approved after appeal; expedited requests show 97.06% approved and 2.94% denied; approval after review extension is N/A. Request counts and the appeal denominator are not shown. Receipt-to-determination means are 3.06 days standard and 0.54 days expedited; both medians are reported as 0 days. These are elapsed times, not reviewer labor or distributions. The CMS-0057-F 72-hour expedited / 7-calendar-day standard deadlines are policy limits. They generally apply from January 1, 2026 to impacted payers other than QHP issuers on FFEs; the rule’s PA policies exclude drugs. They are not measured durations. EMTALA permits insurance questions at check-in only when they do not delay screening or treatment, and requires screening even without insurance.

**Proposed simulation use:** store eligibility, plan/product, urgency, notification, authorization, service delivery, claim submission/adjudication, appeal and payment as distinct states. The sources here support only selected portions of that model. Unknown deductibles, network status, covered services and payment must remain unknown for the earlier Aetna fracture example. The insurer name alone cannot determine them. Emergency screening and treatment must not wait on insurance processing. The EMTALA boundary applies to emergency departments in the law’s scope; it does not determine later coverage or payment.

Still needed: specific member-plan benefit documents and service criteria; bounded de-identified PA event histories; service-specific approval/appeal results; claim reason codes and remittance data; network and contract terms; local financial-counseling workflow. An external multicenter TBI cohort found insurance processing/authorization was cited in 109/210 delayed discharges (51.9%, rounded by the paper to 52%; 210 delayed among 1,543 patients; reasons overlap). This is a post-acute discharge barrier among delayed TBI patients, not a PA turnaround measure. For Luna-facing flows, present these as sourced process facts, keep member-specific benefits/payment unknown until plan documents are checked, and never gate emergency screening or treatment on PA. No patient-specific coverage or payment decision was made.
