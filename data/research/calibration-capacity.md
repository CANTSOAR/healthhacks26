# Room turnaround and staffed-capacity calibration evidence

These nine records add external reference points for occupied-room cleaning, terminal ICU cleaning, procedure-room turnaround, workflow delay, service capability and scheduled staff availability. Every duration retains its source-defined start and end. The measured cleaning durations do not establish a general vacant-to-ready distribution: room departure, notification, EVS response, cleaning, inspection/release and receiving-unit acceptance are separate events and need local timestamps.

In a Baltimore academic center, 89 observed occupied-room cleans had median entry-to-exit elapsed time 14 minutes (IQR 12–19); 50 of 89 had at least one interruption (reported 56%; calculated 56.2%). This is not a hands-on-only duration and describes occupied daily service, not discharge turnaround. In the SHINE ICU trial, the authors define their room turnaround measure as time from cleaning process start to cleaning process end. Median terminal-cleaning interval was 42 minutes (IQR 26–60.5) at baseline, 46.5 during UV/F and 43 during ATP monitoring. The timed-event denominator was not reported in inspected sections. Neither measure includes vacancy-to-clean-start waiting or clean-end-to-ready delay.

In one Toronto outpatient endoscopy unit, the 750-procedure cohort had median non-procedure time (scope-out to next scope-in) 19 minutes (IQR 16–22), while patient-out to next-patient-in turnover time was 6 minutes (IQR 4–9). These are different endpoints; the broader one includes interval work and delay, not cleaning alone. The authors excluded turns over 25 minutes (often scheduled breaks) from analyses requiring consecutive provider pairs, so this is not a break-inclusive operational distribution. A separate UK clinician survey found direct-from-ED MRI capability at 11 of 87 centers (13%) for a specific suspected-fracture pathway. This is reported service capability, not the probability a scanner or slot is free now.

A single telemetry-unit before/after study abstract reports approximately 18% lower bed turnaround after introducing a dedicated discharge nurse, but its description conflicts: it defines the interval as patient escort-out to bed-ready while also saying the measure includes discharge duration, which precedes escort-out. Quantitative use is blocked pending the full paper. The source still motivates timestamping departure, housekeeping wait, cleaning and ready state separately.

For resource modeling, HRSA defines a staffed bed as both licensed and physically available with staff on hand, including occupied and unoccupied beds. This still does not mean a bed is currently empty, clean, appropriate for a given patient, or available at a particular instant. Keep physical/licensed inventory, staffed complement, occupancy, cleaning/maintenance holds and patient-compatible availability distinct. The ACE-unit DES source schedules meal/rest breaks and shift huddles as model inputs; it does not show that breaks were taken or establish a universal schedule. If included, represent break coverage and concurrent availability explicitly.

## Sources inspected

- Xie et al., 2018, [Improving daily patient room cleaning](https://doi.org/10.1080/24725838.2018.1487348). Primary full text; direct observation of 89 occupied-room cleans at one Baltimore center.
- SHINE investigators, 2022, [Stopping Hospital Infections With Environmental Services](https://doi.org/10.1093/cid/ciac070). Primary full text; multicenter ICU cluster-randomized crossover trial, with terminal-cleaning time measured at two hospitals.
- [Efficiency in the Endoscopy Unit: Can We “Turn Around” Room Turnover?](https://doi.org/10.1093/jcag/gwac005). Primary full text; 750 outpatient procedures; reports separately defined scope-out-to-scope-in and patient-out-to-patient-in endpoints.
- [The management of suspected scaphoid fractures in the UK](https://doi.org/10.1302/2633-1462.211.BJO-2021-0146). Primary full text; clinician survey at 87 centers; reports MRI service capability, not contemporaneous equipment availability.
- Salinkar et al., 2010, [Streamlining the discharge process with a dedicated discharge nurse](https://researchconnect.suny.edu/en/publications/streamlining-the-discharge-process-with-a-dedicated-discharge-nur/). Primary conference abstract; single telemetry unit; full sample and absolute distributions unavailable in the inspected abstract.
- HRSA, [Staffed-bed reporting definition](https://www.hrsa.gov/about/faqs/non-financial-data-what-was-considered-staffed-bed-reporting-facility-metrics). Official definition, not a measured capacity series.
- Simmons et al., 2025, [Objective nurse staffing method for an ACE unit](https://doi.org/10.1111/jgs.19507). Primary DES study; scheduled breaks/huddles are model assumptions informed by local clinical input, not measured attendance.

All numerical literature values are external references and remain `external_reference_not_RWJ`. No room-cleaning or staffing value here establishes RWJ/New Brunswick live operational availability. Source paraphrases in the JSON are concise summaries; the original sources are linked for verification.
