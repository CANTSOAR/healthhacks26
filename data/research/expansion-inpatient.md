These are external, study-specific comparators for future scenarios. They are not measurements of a target hospital. Observed event timing, discharge delay, event counts, and shift-level staffing metrics retain each source's setting, period, denominator, and definition. Inferences below are scenario ideas only; local values remain unknown.

## Scenario use and limits

- Treat the ICU readiness-to-departure interval as a possible post-readiness occupancy component. It can include operational delays beyond bed cleaning; the paper's “immutable time” is not a direct ward-bed shortage measure.
- Keep the trial subset and administrative ICU series separate. Their medians differ and represent distinct cohorts.
- The Jennings table reports a mean 9.95 events/day while listing 3,562 annual events; it does not disclose the count of days in the mean denominator. Preserve the published mean as reported and do not derive a daily rate using 365 days.
- Needleman's 6.9% high-turnover-shift share is based on a relative per-unit threshold (day-shift mean + 1 SD). It is a paper-specific classification frequency, not a candidate absolute target-hospital parameter unless its rule and local distribution are re-estimated.
- Use turnover studies to motivate event-count and timing variability. Admissions, discharges, and transfers have different work demands; daily counts are not nurse-minutes or staffing ratios.
- The findings do not establish any local discharge distribution, staffed-bed capacity, nursing time allocation, staffing sufficiency, or target-hospital mortality effect. Replace external values with local timestamped, appropriately governed operational data when available.
