# External medication and sterile-supply operations references

These extracts support discovery and scenario design for inpatient pharmacy and sterile processing. They are external references only. They do not establish RWJ staffing, inventory, service times, policy, workload, defect rates, or patient-level facts. The normalized records are in [`gaps-medication-supply.json`](gaps-medication-supply.json).

## Main findings

Medication operations should be represented as separate states: order communication and pharmacist verification, dispensing, delivery, bedside administration, monitoring, and medication-history reconciliation. An AHRQ evidence report provides this stage framework. A single-center before/after study of 2,988 orders reported a large reduction in mean order-to-verification time after CPOE implementation; its value is a historical external comparison and cannot parameterize RWJ or the downstream dispensing/administering stages.

A randomized trial across two southern Swiss hospitals documents a three-step admission reconciliation process: a pharmacy assistant establishes a best possible medication history, a pharmacist compares it against the physician-recorded admission list, and discrepancies are communicated for clinician review. In its selected older and/or polypharmacy intervention cohort, 830/866 patients had one or more discrepancies (96%; median 6 per patient). Yet the study found no difference in the selected 30-day unplanned-return outcome between intervention and control groups (39.3% vs 39.5%). Process measures and clinical outcomes should therefore remain separate in any scenario.

Sterile instrument availability depends on more than sterilizer cycle time. External studies describe assembly errors, sterilization documentation errors, missing case-cart trays, and OR pauses associated with instrument problems. CDC guidance describes cleaning/decontamination, inspection and assembly, packaging, correct loading and cycle selection, monitoring, release/quarantine, storage, and distribution. Model those stages and holds as separate resource states; local data are needed before assigning quantities, capacities, failure probabilities, or durations.

## Source and evidence notes

The JSON contains 12 extracts from six inspected sources: four primary study sources (including one conference abstract), one AHRQ evidence report, and CDC guidance. Each record includes the source access level and URL inspected. CPOE evidence was taken from the complete PubMed abstract; no search-index-only finding was retained. Primary papers with accessible full text were reviewed at the relevant methods, results, and limitation sections. Retrieval timestamp: 2026-10-10 18:00:36 UTC.

- [Wietholter et al., “Effects of computerized prescriber order entry on pharmacy order-processing time”](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19635778/), DOI 10.2146/ajhp080303. Observational before/after study, 761-bed tertiary hospital, 2,988 orders. Abstract reports mean prescriber-composition-to-verification time of 115 minutes before and 3 minutes after CPOE; pharmacy-receipt-to-verification time of 31 and 3 minutes. Single-site and historical; downstream dispensing and administration were not measured.
- [Ceschi et al., “Effect of Medication Reconciliation at Hospital Admission on 30-Day Returns to Hospital”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8446815/), DOI 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2021.24672. Randomized trial, 1,702 patients at two regional secondary teaching hospitals in southern Switzerland, enrollment November 2018–January 2020. Intervention workflow and discrepancy burden are recorded in separate JSON rows. The selected cohort and null primary outcome limit generalization.
- [Farrell et al., “Understanding Errors in Sterile Processing of Surgical Instruments That Lead to Need for Immediate Use Sterilization in the Operating Room”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6252925/), DOI 10.1093/ofid/ofy210.2142. Prospective 30-day single-hospital study: 17,348 trays for 1,868 procedures; 86 identified errors. Error counts are not a universal defect rate, and the reported local immediate-use cycle is not generalized.
- [“Observed rates of surgical instrument errors point to visualization tasks as being a critically vulnerable point in sterile processing”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11017563/), DOI 10.1186/s12893-024-02407-1. Direct observation in seven pediatric ORs during summer 2021: 236 errors across 147 cases. Delay status was recorded for 103 error cases; the paper reports an average 10.16 minutes lost per documented case. Missing status and pediatric sampling constrain use.
- [AHRQ, “Enabling Medication Management Through Health Information Technology”](https://www.ahrq.gov/downloads/pub/evidence/pdf/healthit/medmgt.pdf). Official evidence report, 2011; used only for the medication workflow stage map, not local throughput.
- [CDC, “Sterilizing Practices”](https://www.cdc.gov/infection-control/hcp/disinfection-sterilization/sterilizing-practices.html). Official guidance; used for reprocessing stages, equipment verification, monitoring, and release/quarantine dependencies, not local asset counts or cycle times.

## Use boundary

All normalized values are explicitly labeled `external_reference_not_RWJ`, and each source is typed as observational, randomized, official evidence framework, or official policy guidance. The source-specific limitations and scenario-use notes travel with every row. No drug, dose, or treatment recommendation is included. No local assumption or probability distribution is proposed.
