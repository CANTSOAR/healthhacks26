# Rehabilitation workflow and outcomes evidence

The sources expose several simulation-relevant states: early screening/case finding, rehabilitation physician assessment, acceptance to acute in-reach or subacute inpatient rehabilitation, multidisciplinary therapy delivery, functional reassessment, and discharge destination. They cover different care settings and must remain separate external references.

At one Australian tertiary hospital, a weekday proactive rehabilitation screening service ran from May 2017 through February 2019 and logged 4,000 episodes. Screening was designed for day 5 of admission, with weekend deferral; ICU subgroup screening occurred at median day 9 (IQR 7–15; n=1,051). Ten-day time sampling recorded 561 minutes for 158 screenings, or 3.6 minutes each. The article reports 86.9% of acute-ward screenings (n=1,604) in the day 5–7 target window, but the stated subgroup total (n=2,949) does not align arithmetically with that count and percentage; do not use this percentage for calibration pending denominator resolution. Screening time is chart case-finding workload, not a therapy assessment duration. [PReS study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7942267/)

A 13-hospital Colorado retrospective cohort of acute ischemic stroke patients (2018–2021) found an adjusted association between each additional day to initial PT evaluation and lower discharge mobility score (B=-0.129 AM-PAC points/day, 95% CI -0.22 to -0.04). The OT and PT analytic cohorts (n=713 and n=979) overlapped; community discharge was 38.3% and 47.1%, respectively. These are acute-care observational results and do not establish causal benefits or inpatient rehabilitation-facility throughput. [Stroke study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12955715/)

National Medicare IRF data for 490,032 patients in October 2016–September 2017 found that higher discharge self-care and mobility scores were associated with community discharge (OR 1.157 and 1.071 per score unit; p<.001). This supports linking functional status to placement outcomes while keeping both outcomes and case-mix factors explicit. [IRF study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6239689/)

These studies do not provide RWJ-specific multidisciplinary assessment queues, daily therapy minutes, staffing ratios, placement wait distributions, or functional recovery distributions. Use them to define candidate events and endpoint choices; populate local rates only from authorized local operational data.
