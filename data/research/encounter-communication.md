# Encounter communication and closure: external evidence

Retrieved 2026-10-10. These records add measurable communication/transition steps to the encounter map. None is a local RWJ measure or an individual patient prediction. The quantitative study records preserve source denominators where available; inferred uses are workflow hypotheses only.

## Evidence and modeled transitions

- **Interpreter access and consent:** Lee et al. report informed-consent adequacy rising from 29% before to 54% after bedside interpreter phones (152 LEP participants across periods; prospective, nonrandomized). Adequate consent required reported understanding of procedure rationale and risks and having questions answered. The disparity persisted against English speakers. Use this to motivate accessible interpreter connection plus a comprehension/decision checkpoint; language must not define a persona or predict an individual’s choice. [Primary article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5515780/)
- **Capacity and shared decisions:** the evidence collected here does not quantify decision-making capacity or preference concordance. Treat capacity assessment, supported decision-making, alternatives/risks/benefits, patient values, surrogate involvement, and documented consent/refusal as explicit encounter states requiring local policy and clinical judgment. Do not infer incapacity from language, disability, literacy, age, or insurance. The interpreter study covers consent for invasive procedures only.
- **Discharge comprehension:** the randomized Griffey study found higher comprehension of post-ED medication, self-care, and follow-up instructions with teach-back (reported p values <.02, <.03, and <.0001; effect sizes not available in inspected abstract). EM-TeBa’s nonrandomized cohort full text reports at least one comprehension deficit in 49% under standard discharge versus 11.9% with teach-back (483 adult ED patients; 239 control, 244 intervention). The study excluded patients with language barriers. These support a repeat-explain-check loop before discharge; they do not establish improved distal clinical outcomes. [Griffey abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26617669/) · [EM-TeBa article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7513274/)
- **Results pending at discharge:** a two-center retrospective study found only 482 of 2,927 pending tests (16%) were mentioned in discharge summaries, and only 67% of summaries documented a follow-up provider. A single-center alert trial of post-discharge microbiology results found documented follow-up within three days for 28% (27/97) with alerts versus 13% (8/60) control; adjusted OR 3.2 (95% CI 1.3–8.4). The improvement still left most results without timely documented follow-up. [Discharge summary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2726888/) · [Microbiology alert trial](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3445692/)
- **Acknowledgment is not closure:** the Australian ED before-after study reported radiology-report acknowledgment rising from 70.6% (1,167/1,654) to 100% (1,423/1,423) after electronic acknowledgment. This measures receipt acknowledgment, not interpretation, clinical action, or patient notification. The Joint Commission advises a named follow-up owner, receipt confirmation for life-threatening findings, escalation after a defined time, backup coverage, and direct patient contact where action is needed; portals alone are insufficient. [Study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7268495/) · [Joint Commission Quick Safety 52](https://digitalassets.jointcommission.org/api/public/content/77c6421712dc463eae4b2398f9bf01a5?v=e9bc1e0f)

## Suggested encounter closure states

1. Confirm communication needs and connect a qualified interpreter when indicated; verify patient’s questions and understanding during consent and shared decision-making.
2. At discharge, reconcile medication changes, self-care, follow-up, and return precautions using teach-back; resolve misunderstandings before release.
3. For each pending result, record the test, expected result time, named responsible clinician and backup, patient contact plan, urgency, and escalation timer.
4. Track result status through received → acknowledged → interpreted → action decided/completed → patient informed, recording failed contacts and escalation. Keep clinician acknowledgment separate from patient comprehension and completed action.

All values are external hospital proxies. Transfer to RWJ only as explicit, replaceable assumptions after local workflow and outcome data are acquired. These sources do not establish RWJ volume, staffing, local interpreter availability, policy compliance, or patient-level risk.
