# Adult distal-radius encounter: treatment and reassessment evidence

Retrieved 2026-10-10 20:40:01 UTC. Scope is a bounded adult distal-radius/wrist-trauma analogue for the synthetic broken-arm encounter. The sources describe different jurisdictions and care settings; each record is labeled `external_reference_not_RWJ`. This is research evidence, not an RWJ pathway or patient-specific clinical advice. ## Located extracts

1. **Initial examination — NSW Health Emergency Care Institute, Colles fracture tool** (adult ED guidance; revised August 2025; lines 59-68). It specifies radial pulse, fingertip capillary return, and radial/median/ulnar nerve power and sensation. It also calls for cast-care and neurovascular-monitoring education. **Evidence type:** recommendation, not observed care. **Scenario inference:** save a baseline exam and do not silently fill missing findings as normal. It does not specify a timed recheck. [Source](https://aci.health.nsw.gov.au/networks/eci/clinical/tools/colles-fracture)

2. **Urgent escalation and clinic timing — NSW Health Emergency Care Institute** (lines 72-90). Open fracture and acute neurovascular compromise prompt ED orthopaedic review; acute compromise is listed for reduction plus review. Stable Colles fractures may be reviewed in fracture clinic at about one week. **Evidence type:** local pathway targets. **Scenario inference:** escalation, referral, and completed specialist review should be separate events; one week is not a measured local interval. [Source](https://aci.health.nsw.gov.au/networks/eci/clinical/tools/colles-fracture)

3. **Post-reduction reassessment — UK Defence Medical Command upper-limb guidance** (general deployed-care section, lines 70-79 and 108-121; wrist section, lines 323-350). It calls for a documented neurovascular exam before reduction, reassessment afterward, and another check after plaster immobilization; the wrist section names radial, median, and ulnar motor/sensory function. **Evidence type:** recommendation. **Scenario inference:** model explicit post-reduction and post-immobilization checks, with interval unknown. This is a deployed-care workflow proxy, not civilian RWJ policy. [Source](https://cgo.mod.uk/clinical-guidelines-for-operations/treatment-guidelines/musculoskeletal/upper-limb-fractures/)

4. **Decision factors — BOASt distal radial fracture standard** (skeletally mature patients; December 2017; standards 1 and 7-10). Document mechanism, skin, circulation, and sensation; use alignment measures and patient needs; consider that volar-displaced fractures are unstable. In adults 65+, it allows non-operative treatment unless major deformity or neurological compromise. **Evidence type:** professional standard. **Scenario inference:** join exam, images, function, and clinician judgment rather than route by age alone. [Source](https://www.boa.ac.uk/resource/boast-16-pdf.html)

5. **Post-reduction fixation thresholds — AAOS/ASSH guideline** (acute adults; adopted December 2020; PDF p.24). For the commonly studied under-65 group, moderate evidence supports operative treatment when post-reduction shortening exceeds 3 mm, dorsal tilt exceeds 10 degrees, or intra-articular displacement/step-off exceeds 2 mm. **Evidence type:** systematic-review guideline recommendation. **Scenario inference:** make these inputs to clinician discussion, not automatic surgery triggers; functional demand and preferences still matter. [Source](https://www.aaos.org/globalassets/quality-and-practice-resources/distal-radius/distal-radius-fractures-10-13-2020.pdf)

6. **Older-adult outcome context — AAOS/ASSH guideline** (PDF p.25 and p.33). Strong evidence indicates operative treatment does not improve long-term patient-reported outcomes over non-operative treatment for the commonly defined 65+ group. The guideline also says functional demand may explain treatment benefit better than age. **Evidence type:** systematic-review conclusion. **Scenario inference:** retain age, function, deformity, neurological status, and patient goals as distinct factors; this is not a rule against surgery. [Source](https://www.aaos.org/globalassets/quality-and-practice-resources/distal-radius/distal-radius-fractures-10-13-2020.pdf)

7. **Fracture-clinic and imaging targets — BOASt** (standards 4-6 and 12-15). Clinic assessment is targeted within 72 hours. Repeat wrist films at 1-2 weeks are recommended when the pattern is unstable and displacement would lead to surgery. Provide recovery/rehabilitation information and a route for patients to request review. **Evidence type:** UK standard, not measured appointment performance. **Scenario inference:** keep target and actual appointment separate; recovery duration remains unknown. [Source](https://www.boa.ac.uk/resource/boast-16-pdf.html)

8. **Later routine imaging — van Gerven et al., randomized trial** (four Dutch level-I trauma centers; 2019; 386 randomized, 326 analyzed). Omitting routine films at weeks 6 and 12 after initial follow-up through two weeks yielded comparable patient-reported outcomes and similar complication rates (11.3% vs 11.4%); median films were 3 vs 4. **Evidence type:** observed randomized comparison. **Limitations:** excludes Gustilo grade 2/3 open fractures and multiple limb fractures; does not justify skipping early or clinically indicated review. **Scenario inference:** represent follow-up imaging as conditional, not a fixed series. [Article](https://doi.org/10.2106/JBJS.18.01160) · [Inspected full PDF](https://research.vu.nl/ws/files/267197746/Omitting_Routine_Radiography_of_Traumatic_Distal_Radial_Fractures_After_Initial_2_Week_Follow_up_Does_Not_Affect_Outcomes.pdf)

## Modeling boundary

The sources support fields and branching cues for open injury, neurovascular findings, post-reduction alignment, age/functional demand, immobilization, reassessment, and follow-up. They do not establish RWJ practice, RWJ arrival-to-treatment or reassessment durations, a complete fracture protocol, or a single cohort applicable to every wrist injury. Numeric clinic windows above are guideline targets; the trial percentages and imaging counts are observed outcomes. No medication dose or recovery duration is supplied.
