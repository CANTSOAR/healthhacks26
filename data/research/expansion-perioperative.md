# Perioperative operations evidence expansion

This is an external-literature evidence set for scenario design, not evidence about any local hospital. The included studies cover operating-room cleaning/turnover, robotic-room task sequencing, PACU occupancy and transfer holds, and sterile-processing case-cart readiness. Each numeric record is also stored as a standalone row in [expansion-perioperative.json](expansion-perioperative.json), with the source locator, setting, period, sample, measurement basis, limitations, and proposed scenario use.

## Findings and possible scenario use

- In a 25-room academic surgical center in São Paulo State, Brazil, direct observations in May 2008 found mean concurrent cleaning time of 7.1 minutes (101 operations) and mean patient-out-to-next-patient-in turnover of 35.6 minutes (60 operations). These are different endpoints; cleaning overlaps the broader turnover process. [Jericó et al., 2011, abstract and methods](https://revistas.usp.br/rlae/en/article/download/4436/5850/6729).
- In 40 robotic turnovers observed at one tertiary hospital (20 pre-intervention, Jan–Apr 2018; 20 post-intervention, Jan–Jun 2019), the pre-intervention task breakdown reported mean cleaning 27 minutes, instrument setup 15.5 minutes, and RN retrieval of the next patient from pre-op 12 minutes. These task means can seed a robotic OR resource scenario only; they are not generic OR or hospital transport rates. [Improving Operating Room Turnover in Robotic Surgery, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35220451/).
- In an Australian PACU study with 1,417 patients across two phases, overall median PACU stay was 49 minutes; 63/1,417 (4.4%) stayed more than two hours. A delay from lack of an available ward nurse for PACU handover was reported for 6.6%. This supports modeling post-anesthesia occupancy and a possible downstream handover hold as separate components, without transplanting those rates locally. [Hughes et al., 2017, Results](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/7/12/e015149).
- At a 700-bed US academic center serving 31 ORs, direct case-cart observations in a sterile-processing department recorded 108 interruptions, or 13.5 per observed hour. In a separate administrative dataset of 41,799 cases, 3,900 tray defects were recorded (9.3% of cases had at least one defective tray). The paper’s abstract also describes approximately 1%—a different figure that appears to describe a specific defect subset; this extract uses the explicitly denominated 3,900/41,799 result. Neither figure is an OR-delay probability. [Alfred et al., 2021, Results](https://qualitysafety.bmj.com/content/30/4/271).

A simple inferred use is to create optional perioperative downstream branches after an ED encounter: admission to OR -> room clean/setup -> instrument-ready check -> OR transfer -> PACU recovery -> ward handover/transport. For scenarios, use source values as explicit external analogues and permit broad sensitivity ranges; do not infer local surgical volume, staffing, rates, clinical decisions, or actual waits from this literature. Per data/AGENTS.md, all future local parameters require replacement by sourced local measurements or remain assumptions.

## Failed and excluded leads

- A search result for a 2026 before/after OR-efficiency study surfaced values from a single center, but it was not included: it fell outside the requested breadth, added a recent single-site intervention result, and was not needed to support the requested cleaning, PACU, and sterile-processing logistics.
- The 2023 “Surgical pit crew” BMJ Health & Care Informatics study was located, but its public article/PDF endpoints did not return full article content in this research session. Search-result snippets were insufficient to verify setting, dates, denominator, and figure locators, so no numeric values from it are included.
- Several PACU and instrument-processing search results gave only publisher snippets or conference abstracts without complete denominator/setting/period details; they were excluded instead of treating snippet-level figures as observed operational parameters.
