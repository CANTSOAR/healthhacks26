# Calibration report: Mayo Clinic Hospital Rochester (model) (`mayo_rochester`)

Generated by `python -m twin.calibrate` - do not edit by hand. **Every number in the "simulated" columns is SIMULATED**; the targets are the hospital's published figures or national survey tables. This is a model built from public data, not the hospital's operational data.

## What was done

The simulator's default policy (the fixed roster a manager would get if they changed nothing) was fitted so that it reproduces this hospital's own published figures. Only catalog attributes tagged `assumed` and the baseline schedule were changed; measured and literature values were left alone. Search: 6 generations x 256 candidates x 6 seeds (1536 candidates, 30 simulated days each after a 7-day warm-up, common random numbers), best 16 re-scored on 24 fresh seeds, winner checked on held-out seeds below. Wall time of the search 65 min on `cuda`.

Search harness check: the same candidate run through the batched search harness and through the ordinary scenario loader gave IDENTICAL trajectories (1501 patients, 3 days).

## What public data cannot tell us

- **How many clinicians are actually on the floor, by shift.** No public source lists an ED's schedule. The schedule above is the one that, in this model, reproduces the published ED time and LWBS. Fewer providers who each work faster, or more who each work slower, fit the same figures (see the ensemble's spread of providers against encounter time).
- **How long a clinician spends per patient here.** The encounter and re-evaluation times are assumed everywhere and fitted here; they are identified only jointly with staffing.
- **Who is inside the ED salary line.** The cost report gives one number for the ED cost centre. Whether employed physicians, APPs, or agency staff are in it is not stated, and it counts paid hours, not worked hours. The nurse roster is set from that number, so it is uncertain by roughly the width of the salary interval; patient flow barely constrains it.
- **Nursing workload per patient.** Nurse minutes per patient-hour are assumed. With the roster implied by the salary bill, nurses are rarely the binding resource in the model; in the real department they may be.
- **Which beds ED admissions actually compete for.** The published occupancy is the annual average over all inpatient beds. The model has one pool; elective and transfer admissions, unit mismatch (ICU vs ward) and time-of-day discharge patterns are not in it. Boarding is therefore the least certain output of the baseline.
- **How patient this hospital's patients are.** LWBS is published as a whole percent. With the literature patience values, that figure by itself forces short front-door waits. If patients here tolerate longer waits than the literature study's, the same LWBS is consistent with a longer wait tail. The ensemble spans this; the point calibration uses the literature value.
- **The wait distribution itself.** No hospital-level wait percentiles are published. The 90th-percentile targets are a national survey that pools hospitals, so they also contain between-hospital spread.
- **ED admissions per day.** Derived from national admission probabilities by acuity, not from this hospital.
- **What the published visit count covers** (`ed_visits_per_year_cms`, from the data build): 84799 visits / 366 days = 232 per day. All ED visits reported under this CMS number; a certification number can cover more than one campus, in which case this is their sum. This is the figure the model's arrival rate uses. The twin models the visits as ONE emergency department; if the count spans campuses, the real main-campus ED is smaller than the twin and every per-day figure here is for the combined volume.
- **What the published occupancy covers** (`inpatient_occupancy`, from the data build): Annual average occupancy of available beds; a calibration anchor for boarding.
- **How many treatment spaces the ED has** (`ed_treatment_spaces`, from the data build): Published by the hospital's residency programme; how the 75 rooms divide into zones is not published (the split below is by rule). Another Mayo page says 72 beds and Wikipedia 'more than 76'.
- **How many advanced practice providers work in the ED** (`em_apps`, from the data build): A LOWER BOUND on the ED's advanced practice providers: CMS gives no ED specialty for them, so they are found through their own Medicare billing. Visits billed under a supervising physician, non-Medicare visits and suppressed rows (10 or fewer beneficiaries) are invisible. NPPES taxonomy codes add 1 (the taxonomy has no emergency code for nurse practitioners or physician assistants).
- **Acuity and case mix of this hospital**, arrival pattern by hour (national survey shape scaled to the hospital's annual visits), and day-to-day surges beyond Poisson variation.
